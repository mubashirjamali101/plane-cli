# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

This project exists to be useful to whoever needs it. Everyone is welcome to take part —
to report a bug, propose a change, or ask a question — regardless of experience, background,
identity, nationality, disability, appearance, age, or beliefs. We are committed to keeping
participation free of harassment for everybody.

## What we expect

- **Assume good faith.** Most disagreements are misunderstandings about context, not bad
  intent. Ask what someone meant before assuming the worst.
- **Critique the work, not the person.** "This breaks on Windows paths" is useful.
  "You clearly never tested this" is not.
- **Be patient with questions.** A question answered in the README is still a signal that
  the README was hard to find or hard to read.
- **Accept that maintainers say no.** Not every good idea belongs in this tool. A declined
  change is not a judgement of the person who proposed it.
- **Take responsibility.** If you get something wrong or hurt someone, apologise plainly,
  fix it, and move on.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Insults, demeaning comments, or personal or political attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing other people's private information — physical or email addresses, employer
  details, anything from a leaked ticket — without their explicit permission.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional
  setting.

## Scope

This applies everywhere the project is worked on — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, code comments — and to any place where someone is representing the project
in public.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying these standards and are expected to act fairly
and consistently. They may edit or remove comments, commits, issues, and pull requests that
do not fit, and will explain why when they do.

Report unacceptable behaviour privately to the maintainers at
**planecli@mubashirjamali.com**. Reports are handled confidentially; you will not be asked
to argue your case in public, and reporting in good faith will never count against you.

Depending on severity and pattern, a maintainer may respond with a private warning, a
public correction, a temporary block from interaction, or a permanent ban. Maintainers who
do not enforce this in good faith may face the same consequences.

## A note on scope

This is a small project. This document is not an attempt to legislate every situation — it
is a short statement of the obvious, written down so that nobody has to guess and so that
there is something to point at when it is needed. If you are unsure whether something is
appropriate, ask.
